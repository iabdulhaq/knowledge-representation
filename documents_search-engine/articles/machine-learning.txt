As a dominant and transformative subset of Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning (ML) pivots away from rigid, rule-based programming by enabling systems to autonomously learn and improve from data-driven experiences. Machine learning frameworks are generally categorized into supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning, each serving distinct computational objectives. Supervised learning utilizes labeled datasets to train predictive models for classification and regression tasks, striving for high accuracy against an established ground truth. Conversely, unsupervised learning algorithms—such as K-means clustering, Gaussian mixture models, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA)—explore unlabeled data to uncover hidden intrinsic patterns, perform dimensionality reduction, and identify structural correlations. Meanwhile, reinforcement learning trains agents to optimize their decision-making processes through continuous interactions with an environment, maximizing a reward signal over time to achieve highly complex autonomous behaviors.
