Artificial Intelligence (AI) represents the foundational frontier of computer science aimed at creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as reasoning, problem-solving, and strategic decision-making. At the core of cognitive AI architectures lies Knowledge Representation (KR), a dedicated subfield that focuses on how real-world information is structured, stored, and organized so that computational models can effectively process it. By utilizing methodologies such as propositional logic, predicate logic, semantic networks, and frame representations, KR translates complex human knowledge into machine-readable formats. These structured formats allow intelligent agents to inherit categories, deduce relationships, and apply IF-THEN production rules to solve intricate problems. Without robust knowledge representation, AI systems would lack the necessary semantic context and foundational understanding required to transition from merely processing raw data to actually demonstrating intelligent, context-aware behavior in dynamic environments.
